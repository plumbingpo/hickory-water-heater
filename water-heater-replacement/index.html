How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another
  • Rust or corrosion on the tank, fittings, or vent connector
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years
  • A slow leak or damp spot under the tank itself

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Replacement?

A water heater swap seems easy right up until you count the connections. Gas units need a leak-free connection with a sediment trap, a correctly sized and pitched vent that sends carbon monoxide outdoors, and enough combustion air. An electric unit needs a circuit and breaker matched to the element wattage. Each tank needs a properly rated relief valve whose discharge pipe ends somewhere safe, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps to meet local code. Moving to tankless or a heat pump raises the stakes further, with larger gas lines, new vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

A professional replacement covers the whole installation, not just the tank. We size the heater by first-hour rating or flow rate, verify the fuel, venting, and electrical capacity, and add the expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. If your current tank still has good years left and just needs a part, we will tell you directly.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • If you chose tankless, flush it annually to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
  • Clean the air filter on a heat pump water heater every few months
  • Have water pressure checked; above about 80 psi, a pressure reducing valve protects the new heater
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
